A · The host venue

What hosting a bartender means

You lend your bar to a guest for one night. Your licence, house rules and team stay in charge; the guest brings the menu and the energy.

The venue provides

  • One night, bar open to the guest from 6 pm to 10 pm (later if you like)
  • An opening order of the brand before the night, so the menu stays on after
  • Ingredients for the guest menu, from the bartender's mise en place list
  • A run-through the day before to check ingredients and prep garnishes
  • A duty manager and bar back to induct and support the guest
  • A station, ice, glassware, prep space and POS buttons for the menu
  • The guest menu printed or on table cards
  • Promotion to your own guests and socials
  • Service under your licence and RSA rules

The venue receives

  • Pre-sold tickets: revenue for two cocktails per guest before the doors open
  • A guest bartender and a menu built for your room
  • The menu and mise en place list at least two weeks out
  • A ticketed crowd from the Drinks Network campaign
  • Every other drink and plate sold on the night
  • Trade buyers in the room, hosted on the brand
  • Artwork, captions and posts with your venue up front
  • No ticketing admin

C · The bartender

Your menu. Their bar.

Provides

  • A two to three drink menu built around the brand
  • The menu and mise en place list to the venue two weeks before arriving
