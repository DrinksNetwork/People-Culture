Why Does Good Whisky Cost So Much These Days?

By Hayden Wood · 18 May 2024

If you're a true whiskey connoisseur, then you probably share an intrigued by the allure of rare vintages or fascinated by the craftsmanship behind expensive whiskey. But does this all have to add up to costing more?

Why does a good whiskey costs so much these days?

Whiskeys have had a long journey since 2000 BCE, from being used in perfumes and for their medicinal properties to now being the world’s most preferred drink. It was then when, in 1405, the Irish Monks perfected the distillation process and gave birth to the liquor as water of life. Since then, the whiskey-making process has improved, from selecting the best grains to the fermentation and distillation process, fetching the best source of pure water. We don’t realise, but all these factors contribute to the increase in the final price. This blog will review the process and understand why a good whiskey is costly.

Let us understand what whiskey is

Whiskey is an alcoholic drink distilled from fermented grain. The ingredients play a crucial role in the making. Common grains used are barley, corn, rye, rice, and wheat. For the ageing process, wooden casks are used, which is why you see the beautiful gold colour, which adds to the wholesome flavour. Whisky enthusiasts often evaluate the quality of whiskey based on factors like quality of ingredients, production techniques, the ageing process, barrel selection, flavours like complexity and finish, aroma, colour and clarity, distillery reputation, and rarity based on their personal preference.

What are the factors that contribute to the final price of the whiskey?

  • Ingredients – The art of making whiskey involves selecting key, high-quality ingredients. Top-tier distilleries source the finest grains, often grown in specific regions known for their superior produce. This meticulous selection ensures the desired flavour profile and adds to the cost. Apart from grains, water is another essential ingredient. Distilleries use water from natural springs, known for their purity and unique mineral compositions. Securing access to such water sources can be costly; they aim for the highest quality. To get the proper finish, complexity in flavours and rich taste, whiskeys undergo the ageing process in the best oak barrels. The ageing period is over several years, and these barrels are used once for one ageing process. As the whiskey ages, some portion of it evaporates through the barrels. Therefore, the remaining fine whiskey becomes even more expensive.
  • Craftsmanship – To give attention to every small detail, whiskeys are prepared in smaller batches. Hence, to make the best whiskeys, one would hire the best, most experienced, and most skilled labourers who have worked for decades in the distilleries and understand what it takes to produce the best whiskeys.
  • Government regulations – Distilleries must comply with stringent production methods, ageing, and labelling rules. These regulations ensure quality and authenticity, but they also increase production costs. Taxes, tariffs, excise taxes, and import duties are some additional costs that may come into play depending on the region from which you are producing and exporting or importing goods.
  • Sustainability and environmental factors – Weather changes heavily affect the quality of the ingredients and then later impact the ageing process. Distilleries may need to invest in adaptive measures to mitigate these effects, increasing costs. Since everyone is worried about the impact of any production on the environment, the distilleries must adhere to sustainable factors like sourcing ingredients locally, using renewable energy, and reducing waste.
  • Marketing and Packaging – Creating a solid brand image and reaching the target audience requires substantial investment. Distilleries invest in elegant bottles, intricate labels, and luxurious packaging materials to enhance the overall experience. This attention to detail, while adding to the aesthetic appeal, also contributes to the cost.

Final Thought

Understanding whiskey is like understanding the art of making it and the science behind it. The craftsmanship and expertise that goes into making expensive whiskey come only with experience, understanding the importance of grains, taste, color, which barrel, what bottle and packaging to use, what sort of aging process it has to go through, how long it needs to be fermented, and the temperature controls; these factors contribute. Australian and Japanese whiskeys are the market leaders, giving you the finest collection. If you are a whiskey enthusiast or savor a smooth Irish whiskey, then understanding this process can help relate to the cost of the bottle.
