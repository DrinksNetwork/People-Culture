Madonna’s Cocktail Craze and Celebrity Endorsements

By Evelyn Harrington · 29 August 2024

Madonna doesn’t own a liquor brand, but her cocktail preferences and endorsement deals have made her a significant figure in the drinks industry.

Here’s how she did it. 🙆

When you think of Madonna, the first thing that might come to me is her trailblazing musical career, bold fashion choices, or maybe her pop genre reinventions. But did you know that in the realm of celebrity sponsorships, the Queen of Pop has also created quite a mix? From well-publicised partnerships to controversial night-outs, Madonna’s relationship with the drinks business is as multifarious and interesting as her decades-long career.
In 2011, Madonna signed a $6 to $12 million agreement with Diageo, the global beverage firm behind Smirnoff Vodka.
The partnership was a component of Smirnoff’s ❝ Nightlife Exchange Project ❞, an international hunt for the greatest dancer available to accompany Madonna’s tour. But this was a masterful fusion of music, marketing, and mixology, not just a talent hunt.

Why Smirnoff? Well, Madonna is not unfamiliar with a decent drink. Her nightclub image matched one of the top vodka brands worldwide, hence the collaboration made perfect sense. The campaign had her in advertising, pouring Smirnoff at a party, and reminding us that Madonna, 65, can still grab attention—and a big salary—by linking her name with a bottle of alcohol.
How then might a celebrity profit from these alcoholic partnerships? As it happens, quite a lot. Particularly when the face of the campaign is as legendary as Madonna, celebrity sponsorships in the drinks industry may be quite profitable. These alliances capture an audience's imagination and, most critically, their dollars, not just provide a face to a product.

Public presentations, however, may also have two-edged effects. UK Newspapers have covered Madonna’s legendary “trashy” evenings in West Hollywood, including the time she was seen with a bottle of Miraval rosé, looking tipsy but effortlessly stylish. Though some may criticize such adventures, for companies like Smirnoff, these events are priceless. Like their product, Madonna is a reminder of the concept of having fun—albeit in a very disorderly manner.

Madonna has a steady cocktail order for a lady who has reinterpreted herself several times: The Pomegranate Martini. Made with vodka, pomegranate juice, strawberry puree, cranberry juice, and garnished with an orange slice, this vivid cocktail reflects the lady who drinks it as assertive and energetic. Later years saw her brand include this mix, which gave her cocktail legacy yet another complexity.

It’s fascinating to think about the symbolism here. Given some claims that the Margarita is the “Madonna of all cocktails,” what makes her favoured Pomegranate Martini different? Like the drink, Madonna’s career has been a mix of sweetness, tartness, and a little bit of punch, maybe about her ongoing relevance.

Madonna

“Queen of Pop”

Not all celebrity endorsements go as naturally as a well-made drink. Pepsi paid Madonna a whopping $5 million back in 1989 for a commercial using her hit tune “Like a Prayer.” After the advertisement took off Pepsi swiftly removed it under public criticism from religious organisations offended by the song’s imagery. Still, despite the criticism, Madonna left with her earnings unconcerned by the incident.

This episode draws attention to the potential risks involved with celebrity endorsements. Although they may be rather lucrative, they also run the risk of backlash—particularly if the celebrity in issue is as provocative as Madonna.

Though Madonna does not have a liquor brand, her influence on the beverage business is indisputable. She’s shown from her Pomegranate Martini to her rich Smirnoff contract that you don’t have to be behind the bar to affect what people drink. She is still mixing music with alcohol advertising at 65, reminding us that the Queen of Pop still rules supreme in terms of celebrity endorsements.

Thus, the next time you drink a cocktail, raise a glass to Madonna—a lady who can create martinis and music with equal flare.
